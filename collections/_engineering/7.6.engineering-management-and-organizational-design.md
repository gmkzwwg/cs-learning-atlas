# English

Engineering management organizes the conditions under which people can build, maintain, and operate software together. It includes setting direction, allocating capacity, developing skills, clarifying decisions, and resolving obstacles across team boundaries. Technical understanding matters because these decisions affect the work the team is actually trying to do.

Consider a team that completes implementation quickly but waits days for the only person who understands deployment. Asking everyone to code faster does not address that constraint. Training another person, documenting recovery, simplifying the release process, or protecting review time may improve delivery more than increasing the amount of code started. Management connects such observations with changes in responsibilities and resources.

Management is one kind of responsibility, not a compulsory promotion above engineering. Senior individual contributors also lead designs, teach, and coordinate. Managers usually have additional responsibilities for staffing, feedback, and organizational commitments. The boundary varies with team size and context; explicit expectations are more useful than assuming a job title determines every duty.

*Prerequisites: Software process (§7.3), architecture (§7.1), and the practical concerns of testing, operation, and maintenance. Experience in a small team is helpful; formal management authority is not necessary to learn how coordination works.*

## From Task Supervision to Team Conditions and Learning

Software organizations inherited budgeting, specialization, scheduling, and supervision practices from earlier industries. Taylor's scientific management emphasized analyzing and standardizing tasks; Drucker's writing on knowledge work drew attention to work in which judgment and information are central. Software contains both repeatable operations and uncertain design work. A reliable release procedure can be standardized while discovering the right product behavior still requires investigation.

McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y and Herzberg's motivation-hygiene theory influenced how managers discussed assumptions, responsibility, and motivation. They are historical frameworks for asking questions, not settled rules that predict every worker's behavior or prove that compensation stops mattering above a threshold. Interpret them alongside the work, incentives, and circumstances of the people involved.

Software-specific experience added another perspective. Brooks described onboarding and coordination costs; DeMarco and Lister's *Peopleware* emphasized working conditions and interruptions. These accounts encouraged attention to how work is organized, rather than measuring only individual activity. Their examples are evidence to examine in context, not a universal formula for staffing or office design.

Hackman's *Leading Teams* organized research around conditions including a real team, compelling direction, an enabling structure, a supportive context, and expert coaching. Edmondson's 1999 study of 51 teams in a manufacturing company found an association between psychological safety and learning behavior. The mechanism to examine is whether people can ask for help, report mistakes, and discuss uncertainty early enough for the team to respond.

Google's Project Aristotle studied 180 teams, including engineering and sales teams. It highlighted psychological safety alongside dependability, clarity, meaning, and impact in its measures of effectiveness. This was research within a particular organization, not a randomized demonstration that one factor dominates all teams or makes skills and staffing irrelevant.

Distributed work and larger software estates make access to information, decision records, and ownership especially visible management concerns. Remote teams need usable written context and deliberate opportunities for discussion; co-located teams can need the same things. Practitioner books such as *The Manager's Path* and *An Elegant Puzzle* supply cases and techniques for these responsibilities, while local outcomes still need to be observed.

## The Management Role, Team Effectiveness, and Organizational Structure

## What Engineering Managers Do

Set direction in terms of a problem and constraints. “Improve checkout” leaves many incompatible interpretations; reducing abandoned reservations while preserving payment correctness gives the team a clearer basis for proposing work. Discuss what will not fit in the available time and who can change priorities when new evidence arrives.

Allocate capacity realistically. Feature work competes with support, incidents, reviews, onboarding, and improvement. Planning every person's full time as feature capacity creates hidden overload. Inspect recurring interruptions and single-person dependencies before assuming a schedule problem means insufficient effort. A specialist who spends all day answering urgent questions has little time left to remove the cause of those questions.

Develop people through specific expectations, opportunities, and feedback. A one-on-one can discuss an obstacle, a skill to practice, or a mismatch in responsibilities; it need not duplicate the status report. Hiring decisions should use job-relevant criteria and structured evidence about capabilities. Vague “culture fit” can obscure which working behavior is actually needed.

Useful feedback identifies an observation, its impact, and a next step, while allowing the other person to explain missing context. For example, “The last two changes lacked migration notes, so the release reviewer had to reconstruct the schema changes; include a compatibility note in the next change” is actionable. “You are not senior enough” supplies neither evidence nor a way to improve. Follow up and distinguish unclear expectations or inadequate support from a skill or behavior problem.

Managers need enough technical context to assess tradeoffs and recognize uncertainty. They can ask for alternatives, a failure scenario, or evidence from a prototype without making every implementation decision. On a small team a manager may also implement features, but the resulting time tradeoff should be explicit. Delegating a decision includes clarifying its scope, constraints, and available support, not merely transferring blame for the outcome.

## Team Effectiveness and Psychological Safety

Psychological safety means that people can raise concerns, ask questions, or admit mistakes without expecting interpersonal humiliation or retaliation. It is compatible with clear standards, disagreement, and responsibility for repairing problems. A team in which everyone stays agreeable while concealing doubts may be pleasant in meetings and unreliable in execution.

A leader's response to information matters. If someone reports a possible data-loss bug, first establish the evidence and decide how to investigate or contain it. Dismissing the concern because the release date is inconvenient makes future reports less likely. Later review can examine the decision and the process without assuming that the reporter caused the problem.

Reported-error counts require interpretation. After reporting becomes easier, recorded incidents may increase even if the underlying system has not become worse. Conversely, fewer reports may mean that people have stopped speaking. Combine reports with service measurements, repeated patterns, and conversations about how work is done. Neither a survey score nor a count measures team effectiveness alone.

Teams also require the skills, information, time, and tools to perform the work. Autonomy without access to needed systems or authority over dependencies can become responsibility without means. Maintain clear expectations for respectful collaboration and address harmful behavior with specific evidence and follow-through. A slogan about safety does not resolve a persistent working problem.

Evaluate productivity across outcomes, quality, collaboration, and the sustainability of work. A person who removes a recurring deployment obstacle may write fewer lines while helping many future changes. Counting commits cannot capture that effect, and comparing counts can encourage meaningless activity. Use multiple sources of evidence and make clear whether a measure concerns the system, the team, or an individual.

## Organizational Structure and Conway's Law

Conway's 1968 paper related system design to the communication structure of the organization producing it. A practical mechanism is that an interface requires people on both sides to agree about behavior and changes. If agreement repeatedly requires difficult negotiation across a boundary, the software may avoid crossing it or accumulate coordination costs there. This is a useful design pressure to examine, not a deterministic mapping from reporting lines to code.

Organize ownership so that common changes have a clear path. A team can own several services, and a shared platform can support many teams. Domain boundaries, specialist knowledge, reliability requirements, and the existing system all constrain the options. Moving names on an organization chart does not immediately change data ownership, undocumented dependencies, or expertise.

Team Topologies distinguishes stream-aligned, enabling, complicated-subsystem, and platform teams, together with different interaction modes. Use this vocabulary to discuss what a team provides and how other teams use it. Temporary collaboration can help discover a boundary; a stable service can later reduce routine coordination; enabling work can transfer a capability. Treat these as design options rather than mandatory boxes for every organization.

Team size affects coordination, but there is no universal optimum. Five people have `5 × 4 / 2 = 10` possible pairs, while ten have `10 × 9 / 2 = 45`. This counts potential relationships, not actual conversations or meeting hours. Documentation, specialization, shared interfaces, and task structure change how much communication is needed. A “two-pizza” slogan or Dunbar's number does not derive the right size for an engineering team.

Clarify decision rights at important boundaries: who decides, who must be consulted, what evidence is needed, and when disagreement should be escalated. Record enough context that people in another time zone can act without repeating the entire discussion. Review the structure using actual change paths and waiting times, including the transition cost of any reorganization.

## What Studying This Changes

You learn to distinguish a person's activity from the conditions affecting the team's result. A delayed release may involve unclear priorities, missing expertise, unreliable tests, or an overloaded reviewer. Naming the mechanism makes a useful intervention more likely than assigning a broad label such as “low ownership.”

In a project you know, trace one decision that repeatedly waits. Identify the decision owner, required information, and missing authority or capability. Propose one limited change, such as documenting a routine decision or training a second reviewer, then inspect whether the waiting and error patterns change. You can practice this analysis without managing anyone.

You also learn to read management advice as a proposal with assumptions. Ask what work and team it describes, what evidence supports it, and how success would be recognized locally. Clear responsibilities, useful feedback, and workable coordination can be evaluated more concretely than a claim that one style always produces high-performing teams.

## Traps

| Trap | Why it misleads | Better response |
|---|---|---|
| Treating management as the next engineering rank | The responsibilities differ, and technical leadership also exists outside management. | Choose and clarify the role by the work it requires. |
| Ranking people by commits, lines, or story points | These counts omit difficulty, quality, enabling work, and different opportunities. | Use contextual evidence about outcomes, collaboration, and growth. |
| Equating safety with agreement or low standards | Concealed concerns can coexist with pleasant meetings. | Invite disagreement, examine evidence, and maintain clear expectations. |
| Applying Conway’s Law as a staffing formula | Communication influences design without prescribing one team per service. | Trace actual dependencies and choose ownership around the work. |
| Giving autonomy without resources or decision rights | A team can be accountable for outcomes it cannot affect. | Clarify constraints, authority, support, and escalation paths. |

# 中文

工程管理组织人们共同构建、维护和运行软件所需的条件，包括明确方向、分配能力、培养技能、澄清决策权，以及解决跨团队障碍。技术理解很重要，因为这些决定直接影响团队实际要完成的工作。

假设一个团队实现功能很快，却总要等待几天，才能找到唯一懂部署的人。要求大家更快写代码，解决不了这个约束。培养第二位成员、记录恢复方法、简化发布过程，或保护审查时间，可能比增加已经开始编写的代码更能改善交付。管理把这些观察与职责、资源的调整联系起来。

管理是一类责任，不是工程师必须走向的更高一级岗位。资深个人贡献者也会领导设计、教学和协调；管理者通常还负责人员安排、反馈和组织承诺。边界随团队规模与背景变化，明确期望比假定职位名称决定全部职责更有用。

*前置知识：软件过程（§7.3）、架构（§7.1），以及测试、运行和维护中的实际问题。小团队经验有帮助；学习协调并不需要先拥有正式管理权限。*

## 从任务监督到团队条件与学习

软件组织继承了早期产业的预算、专业分工、排程和监督实践。Taylor 的科学管理强调分析和标准化任务；Drucker 关于知识工作的论述，则关注以判断和信息为核心的工作。软件开发同时包含可重复操作和不确定的设计工作。可靠的发布过程可以标准化，而发现正确的产品行为仍然需要调查。

McGregor 的 X 理论与 Y 理论，以及 Herzberg 的激励—保健理论，影响了管理者对假设、责任和动机的讨论。它们是帮助提出问题的历史框架，不是能够预测每个员工行为的定律，也不能证明薪酬超过某个门槛后就不再重要。理解这些理论时，应同时考虑工作、激励和当事人的处境。

软件领域自身的经验提供了另一个角度。Brooks 描述了人员上手与协调成本；DeMarco 和 Lister 的《Peopleware》强调工作条件和中断。这些记述鼓励人们关注工作怎样组织，而不只测量个人活动。其案例需要结合背景分析，不能直接变成人员配置或办公室设计的通用公式。

Hackman 的《Leading Teams》围绕真实团队、明确而有吸引力的方向、支持工作的结构、组织支持和专业辅导等条件整理研究。Edmondson 在 1999 年对一家制造企业的 51 个团队进行研究，发现心理安全感与学习行为有关。值得检查的机制是：人们能否及时求助、报告错误并讨论不确定性，使团队仍有机会作出响应。

Google 的 Aristotle 项目研究了 180 个团队，既包括工程团队，也包括销售团队。在它采用的效能指标中，心理安全感与可靠履约、清晰度、意义和影响一起受到重视。这是在特定组织内部开展的研究，不是通过随机实验得出“某一个因素支配所有团队”，也不意味着技能和人员安排无关紧要。

分布式协作和更大的软件系统，使信息获取、决策记录和责任归属成为突出的管理问题。远程团队需要可用的书面背景和有意识安排的讨论机会；同地办公团队也可能有相同需要。《The Manager's Path》和《An Elegant Puzzle》等实践著作提供了相关案例与方法，但实际结果仍需要本地观察。

## Management Role、Team Effectiveness 与 Organizational Structure

## Engineering Managers 做什么

应围绕问题与约束明确方向。“改进结算”可能产生许多不相容的理解；“减少被放弃的预约，同时保持支付正确”，则为团队提出工作方案提供了更清晰的依据。还需要讨论哪些内容无法在现有时间内完成，以及出现新证据时谁有权调整优先级。

能力安排需要符合实际。功能开发与支持、事故处理、审查、新人上手和改进争夺同一份时间。如果把每个人的全部时间都计划成功能开发能力，就会形成隐藏的超负荷。不能直接把进度问题归结为努力不足，应先检查重复中断和单人依赖。专家如果整天回答紧急问题，就很难有时间消除这些问题反复出现的原因。

通过明确期望、提供机会和反馈来培养成员。一对一沟通可以讨论障碍、需要练习的能力或职责错配，不必重复状态汇报。招聘决定应使用与岗位有关的标准，以及关于能力的结构化证据。模糊的“文化契合”可能掩盖团队真正需要什么工作行为。

有用的反馈指出观察、影响和下一步，并允许对方解释缺失背景。例如：“最近两次修改没有迁移说明，发布审查者不得不重新梳理数据结构变化；下次请补充兼容性说明”，就是可行动的反馈。“你还不够资深”既没有证据，也没有改进路径。后续还需跟踪，并区分期望不清、支持不足与技能或行为问题。

管理者需要足够技术背景来评估权衡、识别不确定性。可以要求提供备选方案、失败场景或原型证据，而不必决定每个实现细节。小团队中的管理者也可能参与功能实现，但应明确这会占用哪些其他工作时间。委派决策包括说明范围、约束与可获得的支持，而不是只把结果责任推给别人。

## Team Effectiveness 与 Psychological Safety

心理安全感意味着，人们提出担忧、询问问题或承认错误时，不必预期遭到人际羞辱或报复。它与明确标准、不同意见和修复问题的责任相容。如果大家开会时一团和气，却隐瞒疑虑，团队仍可能在执行中不可靠。

领导者怎样回应信息很重要。如果有人报告可能导致数据丢失的缺陷，应先确认依据，再决定怎样调查或控制影响。因为发布日期不方便就否定担忧，会降低以后有人报告的可能性。后续复盘可以检查决策与过程，而不预先认定报告问题的人造成了问题。

错误报告数量需要解释。报告变得容易后，即使系统本身没有变坏，记录的事故也可能增加；报告减少则可能意味着人们不再开口。应结合服务测量、反复出现的模式，以及对实际工作方式的讨论。问卷分数或计数都不能单独衡量团队效能。

团队还需要完成工作所需的技能、信息、时间和工具。如果没有必要的系统访问能力，也无权处理关键依赖，那么所谓自主可能只是有责任却无条件。应明确尊重协作的要求，并依据具体证据持续处理有害行为。安全感的口号不能解决长期存在的工作问题。

应从结果、质量、协作和工作可持续性等方面评价生产率。消除反复部署障碍的人可能写了更少代码，却帮助了许多后续修改。提交数量无法反映这一作用，用它比较个人还可能鼓励无意义活动。应使用多种证据，并明确指标衡量的是系统、团队还是个人。

## Organizational Structure 与 Conway's Law

Conway 在 1968 年的论文中，把系统设计与构建它的组织的沟通结构联系起来。一个实际机制是：接口两侧的人需要对行为和变化达成一致。如果跨某个边界的协商总是困难，软件可能回避跨越这个边界，或在此积累协调成本。这是值得检查的设计压力，而不是从汇报关系到代码的确定性映射。

组织责任归属时，应使常见修改有清楚路径。一个团队可以拥有多个服务，共享平台也可以支持多个团队。领域边界、专业知识、可靠性要求和既有系统共同限制可选方案。在组织图上移动名字，不会立刻改变数据归属、未记录的依赖或专业知识分布。

《Team Topologies》区分面向价值流、赋能、复杂子系统和平台团队，并讨论不同交互方式。可以用这些术语说明团队提供什么，以及其他团队怎样使用它。临时协作可以帮助发现边界；稳定服务可以随后减少日常协调；赋能工作可以转移能力。它们是设计选项，而不是每个组织必须填满的方框。

团队规模会影响协调，但没有通用最优值。五个人有 `5 × 4 / 2 = 10` 对可能的两两关系，十个人则有 `10 × 9 / 2 = 45` 对。这计算的是潜在关系，不是实际对话次数或会议时长。文档、专业分工、共享接口和任务结构会改变所需沟通量。“两个披萨”的口号或 Dunbar 数，都不能推导出工程团队的正确人数。

在重要边界上，应说明谁决定、必须咨询谁、需要什么证据，以及何时升级处理分歧。保留足够背景，使另一个时区的人无需重做全部讨论就能行动。通过实际修改路径和等待时间评估组织结构，也要纳入重组本身的过渡成本。

## 学习这一部分会改变什么

你会区分一个人的活动与影响团队结果的条件。发布延迟可能涉及优先级不清、缺少专业知识、测试不可靠，或审查者超负荷。指出具体机制，比贴上“责任心不足”一类笼统标签，更可能带来有效干预。

在熟悉的项目中，追踪一个反复等待的决定。找出决策负责人、所需信息，以及缺少的权限或能力。提出一项有限改动，例如记录常规决策办法或培养第二位审查者，再观察等待与错误模式是否改变。无需管理任何人，也能练习这种分析。

你也会把管理建议当成带有假设的提案：它描述什么工作和团队，有什么证据，在本地怎样判断成功？明确职责、有用反馈与可行协调，比“某种风格总能产生高效团队”更容易具体评价。

## 陷阱

| 误区 | 为什么会误导 | 更好的做法 |
|---|---|---|
| 把管理当成工程师的下一级晋升 | 职责有所不同，技术领导力也存在于管理岗位之外。 | 依据实际工作选择并明确角色。 |
| 按提交数、代码行数或故事点评价个人 | 这些计数遗漏难度、质量、帮助他人的工作和机会差异。 | 结合背景，考察结果、协作和成长的证据。 |
| 把安全感等同于一致赞同或低标准 | 愉快会议也可能伴随被隐藏的担忧。 | 允许不同意见，检查证据，并保持明确期望。 |
| 把 Conway 定律当成人员配置公式 | 沟通影响设计，但没有规定一个团队必须对应一个服务。 | 追踪实际依赖，根据工作选择归属。 |
| 只给自主责任，不给资源和决策权 | 团队可能要为无法影响的结果负责。 | 明确约束、权限、支持和升级处理路径。 |
